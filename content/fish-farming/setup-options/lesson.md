## so-01: Ponds are the most common setup
sources: worldfish-2023-baseline

In the WorldFish survey of catfish farmers in Delta and Ogun states, earthen ponds were the main type of facility. An earthen pond is dug into the ground and holds water in the soil, with banks made from the dug-out earth.

## so-02: Where to put a pond
sources: fao-hb-ch2

FAO's beginner handbook suggests a sunny spot close to home, where the fish are easy to look after and less likely to be stolen. A gentle slope is best, because it needs less digging and drains more easily. Avoid low ground that floods in the rainy season, and steep hillsides.

## so-03: How big should a first pond be?
sources: fao-hb-ch3

FAO suggests starting with a pond larger than 300 square metres, which can produce fish for your household and some to sell. Its worked example is a 20 by 20 metre pond, which covers 400 square metres. Square or rectangular ponds are the simplest to build, but another shape can be used if it suits the land.

## so-04: How deep?
sources: fao-hb-ch3

In FAO's 20 by 20 metre example, the pond bottom sits about 1.1 metres below the top of the banks at the shallow end and about 1.4 metres at the deepest point. For bigger ponds, FAO gives depths of at least 0.75 metres at the shallow end and at least 1 to 1.2 metres at the deepest point. The banks are about 1.5 metres wide at the top.

## so-05: Building takes real work
sources: fao-hb-ch3, fao-hb-ch1

Building an earthen pond means marking it out with pegs and string, clearing plants and topsoil, digging in layers, and packing the soil into firm banks. FAO notes that neighbours can share the digging to make the work lighter and faster. This matches its wider point that fish farming takes as much work as growing crops.

## so-06: Tanks you will also see
sources: commons-concrete-tank, commons-tarpaulin-tanks

Besides earthen ponds, you will also see catfish raised in concrete tanks and in tarpaulin (collapsible) tanks set on metal frames, like the ones in the photos here. ## so-07: Matching the setup to your situation
sources: fao-hb-ch1, fao-hb-ch2

Whichever setup you consider, the same basics apply: enough space, a reliable water supply, time to look after the fish, and a way to cover costs. Thinking about these first, before choosing between a pond or a tank, will save you money and disappointment.
