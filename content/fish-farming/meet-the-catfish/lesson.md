## mc-01: The fish most Nigerian farmers raise
sources: fao-2023-catfish-story, fao-fish4acp-nigeria

The African catfish (scientific name Clarias gariepinus) is the fish most commonly farmed in Nigeria, and catfish is the most popular fish on the local market. FAO describes Nigeria as the world's largest producer of African catfish. Getting to know the fish itself helps you understand why so many farmers choose it.

## mc-02: Head and barbels
sources: fao-tp362-anatomy

The African catfish has a flattened, heavily boned head. Around its mouth are four pairs of whisker-like feelers called barbels: one pair near the nostrils, one pair on the upper jaw (the longest and most mobile), and two pairs under the lower jaw. The barbels mainly help the fish find food.

## mc-03: Body, fins and skin
sources: fao-tp362-anatomy

Its body is long and cylindrical, almost eel-like. The fin along its back (dorsal fin) and the fin underneath (anal fin) are very long, reaching nearly to the tail, and are made only of soft rays. The first ray of each side fin (pectoral fin) is a stiff spine. The skin is smooth with no scales, usually dark and marbled from greyish olive to almost black on the back and sides, and lighter underneath.

## mc-04: A fish that can breathe air
sources: fao-tp362-anatomy, belao-2011-air-breathing

Behind its gills, the African catfish has an extra breathing organ: a pair of air chambers holding tree-like structures full of blood vessels that take oxygen straight from the air. FAO notes this lets the fish survive out of water for many hours, or for weeks in muddy marshes. Research shows the fish uses air-breathing as an extra option: it breathes air more often as oxygen in the water falls. This is part of why the catfish is so hardy, but good water still matters (see the water topic).

## mc-05: What fingerlings are
sources: fao-tp362-nursing

Farmers usually start with young fish called fingerlings, bought from a hatchery or raised from tiny larvae. In FAO's nursery trials, larvae were stocked into ponds about 3 days after hatching. About four to five weeks later, most had grown into small fingerlings of roughly 2 to 3 grams, while a minority had grown into large fingerlings of roughly 8 to 10 grams.

::figure catfish-young

## mc-06: Why size differences matter
sources: fao-tp362-nursing

Young African catfish do not all grow at the same speed. FAO warns that when large and small fingerlings are kept together, the larger ones start eating the smaller ones (cannibalism). In its trials, survival fell sharply when fingerlings were left to grow beyond about 15 grams before harvest. This is one reason farmers pay attention to fingerling size when buying or raising young fish.

## mc-07: Why catfish is a common choice
sources: fao-tp362-anatomy, belao-2011-air-breathing, fao-2023-catfish-story

Several things make the African catfish a common choice in Nigeria: it is hardy and can breathe air when oxygen in the water is low, there is strong local demand for it, and young fish became widely available after government hatcheries started supplying fingerlings in the 1990s. Being hardy does not make it effortless: feed costs and good care still decide whether farming succeeds.
